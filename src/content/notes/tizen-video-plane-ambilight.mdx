The usual ambilight is an HDMI splitter feeding a capture card feeding a small computer. So I went the other way. The TV is a Samsung S84F on Tizen 9, and a .NET app running *on the TV* can read the TV's own memory. [TizenAmbilight](https://gitlab.com/ritwick7/TizenAmbilight) reads the decoded video frame, reduces it to perimeter colours, and drives a WLED strip over UDP. One process, one hop, 20 to 25 frames a second.

## The frame that follows the film

Samsung's capture API, which [HyperTizen](https://github.com/reisxd/HyperTizen) builds on, is the wrong layer: it returns the **UI compositor**, which goes static the moment a film starts playing. The plane that follows playback is the **video plane**, and `secvideo_api_capture_video_main` in `libvideo-capture.so.0.1.0` reads it. It fills caller-owned buffers with NV12 at 960×540 and returns a code.

| rc | meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | success |
| -1 | "not ready"; self-clears, a short burst after app start is normal |
| -2 | scaler failure; a wedge, the TV needs a power cycle |
| -4 | DRM-protected content |

## Each LED gets its own patch of the edge

The strip has 73 zones: 23 across the top, 13 down each side, 24 across the bottom. Each averages a border band 8% of the frame dimension deep, and the centre of the picture is never sampled. The lazy version samples the full active height for each side zone, so every LED on an edge is the same colour by construction. The figure at the top shows it.

## Colour: the plane is PQ, the LEDs are not

The video plane carries HDR codes in PQ, SMPTE ST 2084. Decode those as if they were SDR and you throw away the upper half of the signal.

<PqToLed />

- **Tone-map the luma, then scale R, G and B by that one factor.** Doing it per channel invents saturation: +18% mean, and a hue shift on the most saturated zones.
- **Convert BT.2020 to 709.** Feeding 2020 triples straight through renders them less saturated; converting measured a +63% chroma spread. Out-of-gamut colours are desaturated by the smallest amount, not clamped.

## Protected video leaves about 100 readings a second

A streaming app answers -4: the plane is withheld, and the app does not try to get around that. After three in a row it falls back to the TV's own colour meter, which returns the mean colour of one small block of the screen. The TV reports 2 measure slots and a 20 ms settle time, so a finer picture is a slower one.

<Ceiling />

The frame rate you see is interpolation; the real rate is how often each spot is read.

## One datagram per frame, one sender

WLED's realtime DRGB protocol is last-packet-wins, so exactly one sender may own the strip. A single blackout datagram expires and WLED reverts to its stored colour, so every path that blanks the strip re-sends.

## Failing where you can see it

- **A stale frame is not a live frame.** A paused video leaves its last frame in the plane, so capture keeps succeeding while the picture is frozen. A cheap signature detects it and fades the strip out.
- **Capture success is not evidence the pipeline works.** Every safety mechanism reports its own counter in telemetry. A fix has to be verifiable, not asserted.

## What bit me

**Installing over a capturing app wedges the TV.** `tizen install` SIGKILLs the running app, and killed mid-capture it can leave the scaler allocated: every later capture returns -2 until the TV is power-cycled. Three out of three deploys, which is why a `pause` token exists.

**Letterbox detection should be off on at least this panel.** The plane carries no letterbox rows, so the row test can only produce false positives: I watched it crop 25% of the picture. One flag drove both axes; there is now a switch for each, and row detection is off by default.

**There is no auto-restart on crash.** That is on purpose: resuming on a timer risks installing over a live capture, which is worse than a dark strip.

## Tests

The app has zero NuGet dependencies, so its PNG and JPEG decoders live in the repository. A host harness byte-compares the JPEG decoder against libjpeg-turbo, worst per-channel error 3/255.

## Limits

The capture ABI is reverse-engineered against one firmware on one model, and a firmware update is a real risk. A changed ABI shows up as `UNAVAILABLE`, not a sheared picture. The fallback's colours have no HDR decode, and their accuracy against the plane is unmeasured.

The code was written with a coding agent under my direction; the design and every on-device verification are mine.

The idea and the DRGB protocol come from [Hyperion](https://github.com/hyperion-project/hyperion.ng), [HyperHDR](https://github.com/awawa-dev/HyperHDR) and [HyperTizen](https://github.com/reisxd/HyperTizen).
